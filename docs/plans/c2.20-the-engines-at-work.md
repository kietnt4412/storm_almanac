## Why

C2.5–C2.19 made the site look and move well, but every answer still arrives
finished. The optimizer and the pull chain are the hardest work in the
repository, and a visitor never sees them *work* — a plan looks like a lookup
table, a chance looks like a number someone typed. The "how did you make this"
moment comes from watching a real computation respond to your hand.

Both slices are also interview answers: LP duality and why an integer program's
prices are only approximate (C2.20); Markov chains against Monte Carlo, standard
error and Web Workers (C2.21). Everything in them is code the maintainer already
owns or can explain line by line.

## C2.20 · Drag to re-plan

### What the reader sees

- Under the saved plan, a **What if…** panel: energy a day (slider), horizon in
  days (slider), objective (*least energy* / *fewest days*), and each scored
  ladder's reach (the measures the plan form already asks).
- Each **shadow price** row gains a button: *"+10 of this — the price says it saves
  ~N energy. Try it."* (a shadow price is the marginal energy of one more unit,
  per `Explanation`) Pressing it adds the items to a pretend inventory and
  solves; the row then says **what the price predicted and what the solve found**,
  side by side. When they differ, it says why in one line: the price comes from
  the LP relaxation and holds only until another constraint binds.
- The answer is shown **against the saved plan**: the change in days and energy,
  stages that joined or left, runs that moved. The saved plan is never touched.
- **Keep this** runs the ordinary `POST /plan` with the what-if's request (which
  saves it, as today); **Reset** puts the sliders back.
- A small line under the answer: *solved in 38 ms · from cache* — the honest
  proof that this is a live solve.

### Server

- **`POST /api/me/profiles/{profile}/plan/what-if`** — the plan route's body plus
  `extra: {itemId: quantity}`, and **it saves nothing**. A separate route rather
  than `?save=false` on the old one, because a query flag that turns off a side
  effect is one a client forgets once. Quantities are non-negative and capped.
- **`SolveRequest` gains `extra`** (default empty), and `MipOptimizer` adds it to
  the inventory **before** `SolveKey` hashes the inventory, so the cache key is
  right with no change to `SolveKey` itself. Game-agnostic: it is a map of item
  ids, so `GameAgnosticismTest` has nothing to see.
- **`PlanResponse` gains optional `solveMillis`**, wire-optional like every new
  field since the 2026-09-24 release that rendered as nothing.
- **Load:** the solve budget stays 2 s. The page keeps **one request in flight and
  only the latest pending** (an `AbortController` for the stale one, a ~250 ms
  debounce), and the sliders move in steps, so a drag mostly asks questions the
  cache has already answered.
- **Measure first:** time a PGR solve on production (Render's 0.1 CPU) before
  building the panel. If it is not comfortably under ~500 ms, the panel solves
  on release instead of while dragging.

### Tests

- `extra` changes the plan and the key (two requests that differ only in
  `extra` do not share a cache entry); a negative or oversized `extra` is a 400.
- The what-if route saves nothing: the saved plan and its ticks read back
  unchanged.
- One solve against the authored PGR bundle where the shadow price's prediction
  and the re-solve agree, and one where they do not. Both are the behaviour.
- Frontend: a stale answer never replaces a newer one; Keep this posts the
  what-if's request; Reset restores the saved request.

## C2.21 · Watch the odds converge

### What the reader sees

On `/pulls`, under the chance curve, **"Roll it yourself"**. Histories stream
in batches; a histogram of *pulls until the featured unit* builds up, its running
cumulative line closing onto the server's exact curve, with a ±2 standard-error
band and a live caption: *"12 400 histories · largest gap 0.3 points · the chain
says 75.0%, your dice say 74.8%"*. It stops at a fixed count and can be run again.

**The chain is still the answer; the dice are the proof.** Nothing the page
reports as the reader's chance comes from the browser.

### How, without copying the rules into TypeScript

- **`OddsResponse` gains an optional `model`**: the tables `PullModel` already
  computes — the hit rate at each pull count for each wall in the drawn range
  (`hitRateAt(n, wall)`), the wall range itself, and `featuredChanceAfter(losses)`.
  The browser only rolls dice against tables the server sent, so **no game rule
  is written twice**, and a new game needs nothing on the page.
- **The worker draws the wall per cycle**, as `MonteCarloBannerEngine` does,
  while the chain integrates it out (ADR 0023). The two take different roads on
  purpose, and that is what makes them agreeing mean something — the same
  argument that caught the simulation sampling the prior (0.558 against 0.382).
- **A Web Worker**, so 20 000 histories never block a frame; a seeded generator
  so a test can repeat a run.

### Tests

- Server: the tables reproduce `hitRateAt` and `featuredChanceAfter` for both
  published games' banners (acceptance fixtures, not examples).
- Browser: the worker's pure function, seeded, lands within 3 standard errors of
  a fixture curve for a fixed wall and for a drawn one.

## Flashy — pick any

Every effect keeps the C2.17 rules: no animation library, a reduced-motion twin,
paused when off screen, and smooth on a mid-range phone.

| | Effect | Where | Cost | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **The plan morphs.** When a what-if answers, rows slide to their new places (FLIP), stages that leave fold away and new ones slide in, and the days figure rolls like an odometer. | C2.20 | Small — `Count` exists | **Recommended** |
| **F2** | **Pull rain.** Each history falls as a particle and lands in its bin, like a Galton board, and the pile becomes the histogram. The effect *is* the computation. | C2.21 | Medium — canvas 2D | **Recommended** |
| **F3** | **Holo cards.** Unit cards and the dossier tilt toward the pointer and catch a foil sheen: a chromatic glitch shimmer for PGR, gold leaf for R1999. The gacha audience will recognise it. | Roster, catalog, dossier | Small — CSS and a pointer handler | **Recommended** |
| **F4** | **A ripple through the circuit.** Pressing a shadow price sends a pulse from that item through the plan circuit to the days figure. | C2.20 | Small — `PlanCircuit` exists | Nice |
| **F5** | **Weather per game.** A living background: rain that falls *upward* for R1999 (the Storm), drifting embers and scanlines for PGR. Behind the content, never over it. | Every page | Medium — canvas, battery cost to watch | Maybe |

with what the project is about.

## Questions for the maintainer

1. **Which flashy ones?** Recommended: **F1 + F2 + F3**.
2. **What can a what-if change?** Recommended: energy, horizon, objective, reach
   and pretend extra items. **Goal targets are left out of v1**: goals live in the
   database, and overriding them in a request doubles the surface. A later slice
   if it is missed.
3. **Visitors who are not signed in.** Both slices need a profile, so a
   recruiter who does not sign in sees neither — and they are the audience for
   "wow". Recommended: build for a signed-in reader first, then **C2.22, a demo**
   on a fixed sample account that anyone can drag, with stepped sliders so most
   answers come from the cache, and a per-address limit on the solve. That is
   the self-playing demo idea from the sixty-fourth session, made interactive.
4. **Order:** C2.20 first, as agreed. C2.21 is independent and could go first if
   the production solve time (above) turns out too slow for dragging.

**Answered 2026-10-02 (sixty-fifth):** 1 — **F1 to F5**, more than recommended
(F6 stays later); 2, 3 and 4 — **as recommended**. So F5 is in on the
maintainer's choice, with its battery cost on the record: it pauses when the

## C2.20 as built (2026-10-02, sixty-fifth)

**Server.**
- `POST /api/me/profiles/{p}/plan/what-if` (`PlanController.whatIf`) answers
  `{plan, solveMillis, fromCache}` and saves nothing. It always plans on the
  latest version.
- **`fromCache` is read off the plan, not a flag:** a cached plan keeps the
  `computedAt` of its solve, so a plan computed before the request started was
  served.
- `extra` is checked against the catalog (`itemsById`), 0 to 1 000 000 each, at
  most 50 items.
- `SolveRequest` gained `extra`, with a 7-argument constructor so every other
  caller is unchanged. `MipOptimizer.pretending` adds it to the inventory before
  `SolveKey` hashes it.
- `ShadowPriceView` gained `holdable`.
- The plan route's request building moved into `solveRequest`, shared by both
  routes.

**Page.**
- `routes/WhatIf.tsx`, under the answer's card. It's folded to a "Try a
  what-if" line and asks nothing until opened.
- The question is the query key, settled for 250 ms. React Query aborts a
  question nobody watches, and asking one again within a minute is answered
  from the page's own cache.
- "Keep this" asks the plan route with the levers (the plan mutation now takes
  optional levers) and fills the form with them.
- F1: `Odometer` and `useFlip` in `ui/motion.tsx`, with styles and
  reduced-motion twins in `motion.css`. A row that joins slides in and one that
  leaves folds away.
- F4: a pulse drawn once per press in `Ripple`.

**Tests.**
- 3 in `SolveCachingTest`: a pretence is its own key; holding 2 and pretending
  2 share a solve; a negative pretence is refused.
- 5 in `WhatIfPlanTest`: it saves nothing, ticks included; a what-if is not a
  first plan; `extra` solves as held and repeats from cache; bad pretences and
  a missing energy are 400; another reader's profile is refused.
- 4 in `WhatIf.test.tsx`. Frontend 213 → 217.

**Measured locally on sequence 18** (Lacrimosa to every cap plus Samantha's
Resonance, 2 670 Serum):
- A what-if is **44–49 ms on the server and ~90 ms round trip**.
- **A cache hit is still ~35 ms**: the hit skips the solve, not the 15-query
  version load or the naming (`MipOptimizer`'s javadoc already says so).
- Production's 0.1 CPU is not measured yet. `solveMillis` will report it.

**Driven in headless Chrome** at 1280 px light and 375 px dark.
- An odometer showing "11.1" broke onto three lines: the point was a block.
  Fixed with `.odo-digits`.
- The roll was seen as a running `transform` transition.
- **With the Cage at 500 000 the plan is 120 Serum cheaper and 28 days long**,
  whatever the energy: least energy waits four weeks for the weekly grants. It
  is the panel's best demonstration so far, and it was found by dragging.
