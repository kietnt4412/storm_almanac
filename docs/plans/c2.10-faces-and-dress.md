# C2.10–C2.16 · Faces, and a dress with a structure

**Status:** scope agreed 2026-10-01 (sixty-first). The maintainer asked for
"real big enhancement, not minor ones". They were offered seven and took **all
seven, in the recommended order**. Each slice is shown in a browser before the
next one starts, because the faces are a look that somebody has to like, not
something a test can prove. Part of Track C's C2, so C2's exit applies.

## Why

The site is clean, and every screen is made of the same few parts: a dark card
with a corner tick, small text and thin bars. Two things make it generic:

1. **What a reader plans for has no face.** A construct is a name in a box on
   every screen, because game art may not be shipped (CLAUDE.md). Players come
   for the characters, and the page shows them a spreadsheet.
2. **The two game dresses differ in colour and type only.** PGR and R1999 have
   the same layout, the same cards and the same transitions.

## The rules every slice keeps

- **No game art.** Every face is our own drawing, generated from data the
  bundle already publishes: an id, a kind, a rarity, a name.
- **Nothing the solver does not claim.** No day-by-day schedule; the plan is a
  scalar horizon (ADR 0013). A picture draws a number the page already has.
- **One file names a game** (ADR 0039). A game's drawing style is look data
  in `ui/gameChoice.ts`, like its emblem; nothing branches on a game id.
- **Every effect has a reduced-motion twin**, and every screen is driven at
  375 and 1280 px before it is called done.

## C2.10 · Emblems

**The drawing.** A 64-unit square, three layers:

1. **A frame** in the game's style, its stroke the rarity's colour
   (`--rarity-n` by place among the ranks, the rule Inventory already uses).
2. **A motif**, faint, behind: rays, chevrons, broken rings or a lattice.
   The *kind* picks the family, so every construct shares one and every
   weapon another; the *id* picks the variation (how many rays, which cells,
   where the rings break). The same id always draws the same face.
3. **Initials**, in the game's display type: the first letters of the first
   two words of the name.

**Deterministic and tested.** The seed is a 32-bit FNV-1a hash of the id,
fed to a small PRNG; a test pins that one id draws the same markup twice
and that two ids differ.

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `ui/Emblem.tsx`, styled in a new `src/faces.css`. The frame style is the
  look's existing `emblem` field (`plate` / `seal`), with `hex` for a game
  that has no look, so no new look data was needed.
- **Two motifs, not one.** With one family per kind, five constructs looked
  like one construct five times. The id now also picks a fainter second family
  (never the kind's own) and a **serial**: `S4-2Y` on a plate or hexagon,
  `Nº 563` on a seal.
- **Rarity is compared within a kind** (`ranksByKind` in `ui/rarity.ts`, where
  `tierColour` moved from Inventory). A weapon's 6★ had made every S-rank
  construct look lower. Pips under the initials count down from four for the
  kind's highest rank.
- **A missing rarity draws a brand-coloured frame and no pips**, never a
  thrown error: two older test fixtures carried none and took their pages down.
- On: the catalog (a grid with **kind filter chips**), the character page's
  header, roster and goal cards, inventory tiles from `sm` up (on a phone the
  stripe already says the rarity), the plan circuit's targets, and the banner
  cards on Home and Pulls (a banner is its own subject: its type is its kind).
- Driven at 1280 and 375 px on `rehearsal-s6`. The seal and hexagon were
  checked on a static gallery, because the local R1999 catalog fails to load
  (`craft-spell-of-banishing consumes nothing`), which is unrelated to this.

## C2.11 · The hangar

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `roster/UnitCard.tsx`: a tall card per construct. **Front:** rarity and
  kind, the emblem inside a ticked ring that fills with how far up every track
  they are, the name, "N% up every track · M of K at the top", the **next
  rung** (the first track in the game's order not at the top, and that step's
  own price), and where they stand on the first three tracks. **Back:** every
  track as the C2.8 ladder (scrolling past 15rem) and **what is left to the
  top of every track**, `rangeCost` summed with `sumOf`: the steps' own
  prices, never a plan, a step sold at several prices named and not added.
- The card turns over in 3D; with reduced motion it swaps faces instantly.
  The face turned away is `inert`.
- **Edit** opens the existing editor beside the card, the pair taking the
  whole row; "Remove … from the roster" moved into the editor. A construct
  whose graph has not loaded keeps the plain card.
- Two icons added: `refresh` and `edit`.
- Driven at 1280 and 375 px: front, back, and the editor.

## C2.12 · The dossier

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `catalog/Dossier.tsx` heads the character page. **The hero:** the emblem at
  128 px in the portrait ring (moved to `ui/PortraitRing.tsx`), filled as far
  as the signed-in reader has them; rarity, kind and serial; the name at
  display size in the game's dress; element and tags as chips; three tiles
  (tracks, upgrades, the reader's percent or "—"); and **Plan this**.
- **Plan this** links to `/goals?add=<entity>`, which the goal screen now
  reads to open that row. An id the patch does not have opens nothing.
- **The staircase** is the first track in the game's order, a step per state,
  filled to where the reader stands, with the rank families under it. Signed
  out it draws the climb without a position.
- **To the top of every track:** one bar split by track in one currency, a
  chip per currency (the one the most tracks spend first, since currencies
  cannot be added together), and a legend with each track's amount and share.
  The steps' own prices, not a plan, like the goal screen's line.
- Everything under it (the reader's shortfall, step costs, sourcing) is as it
  was. Driven at 1280 and 375 px on Lucia: Inverse Crown.

## C2.13 · The plan card

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `ui/ShareCard.tsx`: the plan as a 1200 × 630 SVG in the game's dress (a
  slash in the corner on a plate, a ruled frame on a seal), with the emblem of
  whoever the plan has the most steps for in a ring, "and N more" for the
  rest, the cost, the days (`daysOf`, moved to `ui/time.ts` so the card and
  the plan print days alike), the runs, the stage with the most runs, and the
  patch it was solved on.
- `toPng` turns it into a PNG on the device: the page's palette tokens are
  resolved and written into the SVG with `faces.css` (imported `?raw`),
  because an image carries no stylesheet. Checked in a browser: 1200 × 630,
  colours, fonts and emblem intact.
- `ui/ShareDialog.tsx`, behind **Share** on the plan's answer card: the card
  as it will be sent, then **Share…** (the phone's share sheet, where it takes
  files), **Copy image**, and **Save as PNG**. Rendered through a portal,
  because the page's entrance animation makes its own box what `fixed` is
  fixed to.
- **Nothing leaves the device until the reader picks one.** No server is
  asked and no link is made.

### Not built: the unfurl — a decision for the maintainer

A link that previews the plan in Discord needs a URL anyone can open: a
**public, read-only snapshot of a private plan**, an image rendered on the
server for the preview, and a way to revoke it. That makes a plan readable by
whoever holds the link, which no route does today (every plan is behind
`/api/me`). It wants an ADR, a `share` table with an unguessable token, and
the maintainer's yes before anything is built.

## C2.14 · Pulls you can feel

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `ui/PullStrip.tsx`, in the pull answer under the headline: a cell per
  pull — first the ones already on the reader's counter (for one copy), then
  every pull to the guarantee — lit as far as the chosen day affords, with a
  dot over the pull nearest the average.
- **The heat on a cell is the chance of landing on exactly that pull**: the
  step in `curve` onto it, which the chain already returns. **No new field
  was needed**, contrary to the plan's guess. On a PGR banner (0.5%, wall 60)
  it is honest and plain: every pull alike, the guarantee bright.
- **A day slider** over `byDay`: its pulls and its chance, with the date. It
  opens on the day asked. A server that sends no `curve` gets no strip.
- Driven at 1280 and 375 px on a local test account, `c214-pulls`, with 7 000
  Black Cards: 28 pulls, 13%.

## C2.15 · The skill tree

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

- `roster/SkillTree.tsx`: the emblem in the middle, a spoke per track, a node
  per state. Sections are sectors, each opened by an empty spoke with the
  section's name written along it (a first try put the names outside the
  circle, where they ran into the track labels). Nodes: **reached** in the
  brand colour, the path **to the goal** in amber and moving, what can be
  **aimed at** hollow, what no step arrives at dimmed and inert.
- **A click aims the track there**; the target again, or any node behind the
  reader, leaves the track as is. A node's tooltip is its price from where the
  reader stands. A panel beside it says the pointed-at track and the row's
  total, by the steps' own prices.
- **It is the ladders drawn another way**: the same inputs as `TargetPicker`,
  the same `onChange`. Checked in a browser: a node set on the tree shows on
  the ladders.
- **On a wide screen only** (1024 px and up), with a Tree / Ladders switch
  remembered in `localStorage`. Below that, and for the keyboard and screen
  readers, the ladders.
- **No locks, unlike the plan.** The upgrades route does not say which steps
  have a gate, so a lock would have been a guess.

## C2.16 · A dress with a structure

### As built (2026-10-01, sixty-first)

All in `faces.css` under `:root[data-game]`, plus a `page` class on the
route's wrapper in `App.tsx`; a game with no look keeps the Storm page.

- **Punishing: Gray Raven as a combat screen.** Scanlines and a vignette over
  the page (`pointer-events: none`, under the dialogs); corner brackets on
  every card at the two corners the tick does not hold; every number in the
  HUD's mono; a skewed bar before each card's heading; and a page that
  **locks on** as it arrives, sliced and shaken for half a second, then still.
- **Reverse: 1999 as a printed almanac.** Ruled lines down the content and a
  red double margin rule; the quiet lines under the numbers set as **margin
  notes** in italic serif; a **§** before each card's heading; a double-ring
  **stamp** pressed into the corner of every answer; and a page that **turns
  over from the left** like a leaf.
- Reduced motion keeps every frame and drops both arrivals. Checked: the
  overlay never takes a click, and the page settles to no transform and no
  clip. Driven at 1280 and 375 px in both games.
