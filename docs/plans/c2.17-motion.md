# C2.17–C2.19 · Motion where the answer is

**Status:** agreed 2026-10-01 (sixty-third). The maintainer asked to improve the
site "in the animation and effect side". After a tour, three slices were
offered and taken in the recommended order, **A → B, C as a stretch**; PGR is to
feel "more like a game", **with no screen shake**; the toolkit was left to the
agent, on the terms "easy to do and to scale up later". Part of Track C's C2,
so C2's exit applies.

## Why

The site already had a lot of motion (C2.6, C2.7, C2.10–C2.16): the opening,
the wordmarks, a word-by-word hero, a rise per route, scroll reveals, the
plan circuit's pulses, cards that turn over. **All of it is arrival.** Once the
page is in, the moment the reader waited for got the least: on `/pulls`, after
an answer came back, the only animations running were the wordmark and the
footer's signature. The dial, the percentage, the curve, the strip and the
copies bar appeared in one frame, and a button sent a request with nothing but
a changed label.

## The toolkit, so a later slice adds motion by naming it

No animation library: the project hand-writes its charts and icons, the
bundle stays the same size, and every piece is something the maintainer can
explain line by line.

- **Numbers: `useTween` and `Count`** (`ui/motion.tsx`) — the one JavaScript
  animation. Counts from `from` on mount and from where it stood on change.
  A screen reader is told the value, never the frames. Still where motion is
  reduced, where there is no `requestAnimationFrame` (jsdom), in a hidden tab,
  and **lands on the value by a timer** in a window that says it is visible and
  draws no frames.
- **Shapes: classes in `motion.css`** — `draw` (an SVG stroke with
  `pathLength="1"`), `bloom`, `pop`, `wipe` (a clip, so rounded ends are not
  squashed), `stagger-in`, each taking `--delay`. **Fill mode `backwards`**, so
  once played the element is its own style again (a `wipe` left holding
  `inset(0)` would cut off a glow). Inside a `Reveal` still waiting to scroll in,
  they wait with it.
- **An answer: `.land` on its card, `.land-figure` on its headline number**,
  dressed per game in `faces.css`. Each answer is **keyed by when it was asked**
  (`run.submittedAt`, `plan.computedAt`), so a new answer lands and ticking a
  line on the same plan does not — which also resets the pull strip's day
  slider, which used to keep its place across answers.
- **Feedback: `data-working` on a button** from its mutation's pending state;
  `.tick-in` for a confirmation.

Timing tokens (`--ease-out`, `--ease-snap`, `--dur-draw`) are on `:root` in
`motion.css`. Every effect has a reduced-motion twin.

## C2.19 · Route transitions (agreed for the next session)

1. **The router.** `main.tsx` moves from `<BrowserRouter><Routes>` to
   `createBrowserRouter(createRoutesFromElements(…))` and `<RouterProvider>`;
   the route tree is unchanged, and tests that render under `MemoryRouter`
   are untouched. Then `viewTransition` on the links that should morph.
2. **The emblem.** `view-transition-name: emblem-<id>` on the catalog tile's
   or roster card's emblem and on the dossier's, unique per page. **Prefetch
   the entity on hover and focus** (`queryClient.prefetchQuery`), or the
   dossier renders its loading state inside the transition and there is no
   emblem to fly into.
3. **The step bar.** The current segment named `step-here`, so it slides from
   one step to the next.
4. **The page entrances.** The `.page` rise, `pgr-lock-on` and `r1999-leaf`
   already play on a route change; decide in a browser whether they play
   inside a view transition or give way to it, so a page does not arrive twice.
5. **Fallback.** A browser without `startViewTransition` and a reader asking
   for reduced motion keep today's entrances.
